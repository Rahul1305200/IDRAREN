Our story

It begins in the Atlas Mountains.

Generations have woven memory, culture and craftsmanship into cloth here. For us, Idraren, meaning mountains in Amazigh, is more than a landscape. It is a feeling of belonging, a reminder of where we come from and what keeps us grounded.

For four generations our family has carried a deep connection to rugs, to artisans, and to the beauty of craftsmanship. Today Idraren is carried forward by the third-generation granddaughter, continuing a story that began long before the brand existed, a journey to honour what came before, preserve what is precious, and carry this heritage into new homes around the world.

Stories woven together.

Every Idraren rug carries more than one story. The story of the hands that made it. The land and traditions that shaped it. And the story of the home where it will continue to live.

Somewhere between those stories, something quietly beautiful happens. A piece of Moroccan heritage leaves the hands that created it and begins a new chapter.

Named for the mountains.

Idraren means mountains in Tamazight, the High and Middle Atlas where our weavers live, dye, and knot. The name is not a flourish; it is the place the work comes from.

We work directly with families who still weave by hand. Each rug carries a region, a rhythm, and a lineage, not a factory calendar.

Every knot holds a name. My grandmother's hands are still in this wool.

Yasmina

Yasmina's family has kept the loom for four generations in the Middle Atlas. Motifs are remembered, not printed; wool is washed and spun close to home; colour comes from patience as much as pigment.

Yasmina, Middle Atlas. Third generation at the loom.
The craft

Wool, knot, time.

From fleece to finished field, every stage stays close to the hand. We do not rush a piece for a season, we wait for it to be ready.

  • 01 Fibre Local wool, washed and prepared for the loom.
  • 02 Colour Natural and traditional dyes, set with time rather than haste.
  • 03 Knot & finish Hand-knotted structure, washed and finished in Morocco before shipping.
Our process

Learning to see the difference.

Over the years the family has built relationships with the finest artisans and weaving communities across the country. We have learned to look beyond what first meets the eye, to recognise the quiet language of a fine rug: the softness and strength of its wool, the depth of its natural dyes, the rhythm of its weave, the harmony of its design, and the human touch held in every knot.

Its real beauty is rarely there at first glance. It is revealed slowly, through touch, time and attention. Two rugs may look alike and carry entirely different souls, learning to see that difference is part of what was passed down to us.

01Curated

Curated, not simply collected.

We do not simply source rugs, we look for the ones that speak to us. Each piece is chosen for its authenticity, craftsmanship, character and ability to endure. Some are vintage treasures; others are made today with skilled artisans, letting an old language find new expression.

We look closely: where a rug comes from, who made it, what it is made of, and what makes it particular. Then we choose the pieces that carry something beyond their beauty. A rug should never feel like something placed in a room. It should feel as though it was always meant to be there.

So we take time to understand the home a rug is going to, the people in it, and the feeling they want to create. The right one can soften a room, ground it, and make it feel more like home.

Perfection in imperfection
  • Choose thoughtfully, value craftsmanship, and favour pieces made to live with you for years rather than seasons.
  • The small irregularities of hand-spun wool, the shifts in texture, the traces left by the maker's hand, these are not faults to be erased. They are what makes each rug singular.
  • A well-made rug is not built for a passing trend. It gathers memories, develops character, and travels from one home to another, one generation to the next.
02Diverse

Rooted in the Atlas, inspired by far more.

Idraren pays homage to the Amazigh rugs of the Atlas mountains, one of the most distinctive weaving traditions there is. But we also honour the many regions, communities and traditions that make up Morocco's textile heritage. From the mountains to the plains, the coast, the desert and the cities, each region speaks its own language of colour, pattern, material and craft. Amazigh rugs are one thread in a much larger tapestry.

03Ethical

Honouring the hands behind the craft.

People before profit. Fairness before convenience. Heritage before trend. Transparency before storytelling. Longevity before disposability.

Every rug begins with a person. Behind each piece are skilled hands, knowledge passed down through generations, and hours given to a craft that cannot be hurried. The people who keep this heritage alive belong at the heart of its story.

Our approach rests on close, lasting relationships with the artisans and weaving communities we work with. Staying connected to the people behind each piece is how we protect not just the craft, but the human story woven into it.

For generations, women have carried techniques, symbols and traditions from mother to daughter, and we honour that by treating their expertise as exactly that, expertise. But weaving has never belonged to women alone. Men have woven and go on weaving, bringing their own skill and knowledge. We honour them all: the hands behind the loom.

Craft has a future when the people behind it do.

04Sustainable

Made slowly. Made to last.

Sustainability begins with making things worth keeping.

These rugs belong to a slower way of making. Woven by hand rather than mass-produced, each one takes time, patience and real human skill. At the heart of the collection is wool, a natural fibre chosen for its warmth, resilience and the way it ages gracefully.

Less, but better. Natural, enduring, made with intention.
